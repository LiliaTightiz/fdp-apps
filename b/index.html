How the coordinated mode works (equations and checks)

Goal of the utility. Keep the total load as flat as possible. This is valley filling. Every EV must still get its energy before it leaves.

min Σ_t ( D_t + Σ_n r_n,t )² Δt s.t. 0 ≤ r_n,t ≤ r̄_n inside the plug-in window, Σ_t r_n,t Δt = R_n

Decentralized protocol (Gan, Topcu and Low, IEEE Trans. Power Systems 28(2):940–951, 2013). In round k the utility broadcasts one signal. Each EV updates only its own plan.

p_t^k = D_t + Σ_n r_n,t^k (broadcast) r_n^k+1 = argmin_r∈F_n p^k·r + ||r − r_n^k||² / (2β) = Π_F_n( r_n^k − β p^k ), β = 1/N

Why it is decentralized. An EV needs only the broadcast signal and its own arrival time, departure time and energy need. No EV reveals its plan to another EV.

Checks. The result matches the central QP optimum (cvxpy with Clarabel) for 10 to 100 EVs, with a gap below 0.0001 %. About 20 rounds reach 0.01 % of the optimum.

Assumptions. 110 homes with a synthetic evening-peak profile. Transformer 250 kVA at power factor 0.95, so 237.5 kW. Home chargers of 3.3 kW and 7.2 kW. Arrivals 16:30 to 22:30, departures 06:00 to 08:00, needs of 6 to 18 kWh. Charge rates are continuous. The cheap window opens at 22:00, and every timer starts at that moment.

Lesson. A price that is the same for everyone moves the peak. A price that answers the total load removes it.
